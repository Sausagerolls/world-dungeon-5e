# Quests

> *"New quest! A goblin child has lost her favorite grenade. It is somewhere in the Birthday Party Wing. It is live. Reward: her undying gratitude, and 40 gold. The System recommends hurrying."*

The World Dungeon is full of people who want things. Locals with problems, shopkeepers with grudges, other crawlers with bad plans, gods with very specific demands, and the System itself, which likes to set tasks just to watch you do them. When someone asks you for something and the System writes it into your Interface, it is a **quest**.

Quests are optional. Nobody has to take one. But they pay in gold, loot boxes and favors, and some of them change the floor around you. A quest you fail usually costs you nothing but the reward. The world, on the other hand, keeps score.

---

## The Quest Log

Every crawler's Interface has a **Quest Log**. When you accept a quest, it appears there with:

| Entry | What it tells you |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Name** | What the System calls it. Usually a pun. |
| **Giver** | Who asked, and where to find them when you're done. |
| **Difficulty** | One to five stars (★ to ★★★★★). The System's honest guess. |
| **Length** | Quick, Short, Floor or Long (below). |
| **Deadline** | When it fails if you aren't finished, if it has one. |
| **Objective** | What you have to do. Some quests have several steps, and the log ticks them off. |
| **Reward** | What the giver promises. The System guarantees System rewards. Locals sometimes lie. |
| **Penalty** | Only if the quest has one (see *Penalty Quests*). It is always shown before you accept. |

Write your quests on the **Quests** page of your character sheet. Everyone in a party can accept the same quest. Each crawler who helps finish it gets the reward (unless the giver only has one of the thing).

### Where Quests Come From

| Giver | How it finds you |
|-------|------------------|
| **Locals** | NPCs who live on the floor: residents of a neighbourhood, monsters who would rather talk, the odd survivor. Ask around. |
| **Shopkeepers** | Safe room shopkeepers want materials, revenge, or a competitor gone. They pay in gold and discounts. |
| **The Quest Board** | Most safe rooms have a cork board of System-posted jobs. Read it on any rest. |
| **The System** | System Quests pop up on their own, often mid-fight: *"Kill 20 rats. Go."* The System always pays. |
| **Other crawlers** | Ask for help, offer a trade, or set you up. The System enforces the reward, not the honesty. |
| **Bosses** | Sometimes a boss offers a deal instead of a fight. Read the fine print. |
| **Your god** | Only if you worship one (see *Divine Quests*). You don't get to say no for free. |

### Accepting and Abandoning

- **Accept** by saying yes to the giver. The quest goes into your log. You can carry as many ordinary quests as you like.
- **Decline** freely. Most givers ask someone else, or ask again later.
- **Abandon** a quest at any time from your log. It counts as **failed**.
- **Hand it in.** Most quests are finished when you return to the giver. System Quests finish the moment the objective is met.

## Difficulty

The System rates every quest from one to five stars. It compares the job to your party's level, not to the floor.

| Stars | Difficulty | What it usually means |
|-------|------------|-----------------------|
| ★ | **Errand** | No real danger. A walk, a chat, a single easy fight. |
| ★★ | **Job** | A proper fight or two, or a tricky problem. |
| ★★★ | **Venture** | Several fights, a mini-boss, or real risk of losing a crawler. |
| ★★★★ | **Ordeal** | A neighbourhood boss, a deadly place, or a job that should need a bigger party. |
| ★★★★★ | **Legend** | A floor boss, a god, or something nobody has done before. |

The stars are the System's guess. The System is usually right, and occasionally hilarious.

## Length

| Length | How long it takes | Usual deadline |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Quick** | One scene: a few floor turns | Often none, or "in the next hour" |
| **Short** | A session, or about 6–12 floor turns | Often a number of floor turns |
| **Floor** | Most of a floor | Before you take the stairs, or before the floor collapses |
| **Long** | Two floors or more | A floor number ("by the end of Floor 4") |

**Floor-bound quests.** A quest whose giver lives on this floor fails when you take the stairs down, unless the quest says otherwise. The floor collapses behind you, and so does the job.

## Rewards

The giver names the reward when you accept. Most rewards are a mix of these:

| Reward | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| **Gold** | Grows with the floor, the stars and the length. |
| **A loot box** | The giver's (or the System's) box, of a type that fits the job. More stars, better rarity. |
| **An item** | Something the giver owns: a weapon, a key, a recipe, a pet egg. |
| **A favor** | A discount, a safe place to sleep, a door unlocked, an ally for the next fight. |
| **A Crawler Skill level or a Stat Point** | Only from the hardest quests, and only from the System. |
| **Favor with your god** | From divine quests only. |

Rough guide to what each difficulty pays:

| Stars | Box | Extras |
|-------|-----|--------|
| ★ | Usually gold or an item; sometimes the floor's usual box | — |
| ★★ | The floor's usual box | — |
| ★★★ | The floor's big-deed box | Sometimes an item or a favor |
| ★★★★ | The floor's exceptional-deed box | Often an item; sometimes a Crawler Skill level |
| ★★★★★ | Legendary | A Stat Point, a Crawler Skill level, or a title |

"Usual", "big deed" and "exceptional deed" are the columns of the *Rarity by Floor* table in `loot-boxes`.

- **No Audience for nothing.** Finishing a quest doesn't earn Audience points by itself. How you finish it might (see `surviving-the-crawl`).
- **Achievements.** A good quest is often an achievement too. The GM decides.

## Failing a Quest

A quest fails when its deadline passes, when the objective becomes impossible (the person you were escorting is now two people), when you abandon it, or when you take the stairs with a floor-bound quest still open.

> *"Quest failed! Don't worry. Nobody is angry. The System is a little bit angry."*

**Failing an ordinary quest costs you nothing directly.** You don't get the reward, and that's it. No damage, no lost levels, no System punishment.

**But the world remembers.** Every quest exists because something on the floor needs doing. If you don't do it, it doesn't get done, and things happen:

- the shopkeeper you didn't help closes up, or doubles her prices,
- the monsters you didn't clear spread into the next neighbourhood,
- the boss you didn't stop finishes its ritual,
- the person you didn't rescue doesn't come back,
- the crawler you let down tells everyone.

You won't always know in advance what failing will do. The GM does.

### Penalty Quests

Some quests do punish failure directly: System dares, contracts with bosses, deals with crawlers that the System enforces. A penalty quest **always shows its penalty before you accept**. If you didn't see the penalty, it doesn't have one. The System is petty, but it plays fair.

> *"DARE ACCEPTED. Kill the Mega-Sweeper 9000 without using a weapon. Penalty for failure: you will be called 'Mop Boy' in every System message until the end of the floor."*

## Questlines

Some quests lead to other quests. A **questline** is a chain of quests (usually three to six) told as one story: find the missing kid, learn who took her, follow them down, get her back. Each quest in the chain is a **link**.

- **Each link pays its own reward.** Finishing the whole chain pays a **Questline reward** on top: usually a better box, a title or an achievement.
- **The next link appears** when you finish the one before. Sometimes the giver tells you, sometimes the System does, and sometimes you just find it.
- **Links can cross floors.** A questline can start on one floor and finish three floors down. Its links are not floor-bound unless they say so.
- **Failing a link** doesn't always end the chain. Sometimes the story goes on, just worse. Sometimes it ends there. Either way, the world remembers.

Note each link of a questline on your sheet as its own quest.

## Divine Quests

*Only with the optional Gods module (see `gods`).*

If you worship a god, your god will sometimes want something done. When it does, the world freezes for a heartbeat and a gold-edged notification takes over your Interface:

> *"DIVINE QUEST. Embrix the Unquenched requires a bonfire. A big one. In the Mushroom Rave. Reward: 5 Favor. Failure: you will be warm. Very, very warm."*

A divine quest is a quest from your god. It works like any other quest, with these differences.

### Accepting

- **One at a time.** You can only carry one divine quest. Your god doesn't give you another until it is done or failed.
- **Saying no costs.** Declining a divine quest gives you **1 Lapse** (see *Taboos and Lapses* in `gods`). Gods don't like being told no.
- **Saying yes binds you.** Once accepted, the only ways out are success or failure.

### Rewards

A divine quest pays **Favor**, and usually a **Divine box**:

| Stars | Favor | Divine box | Extra |
|-------|-------|------------|-------|
| ★ | +2 | — | — |
| ★★ | +3 | The floor's usual box | — |
| ★★★ | +5 | The floor's big-deed box | Remove 1 Lapse |
| ★★★★ | +8 | The floor's exceptional-deed box | Remove 1 Lapse |
| ★★★★★ | +12 | Legendary | Remove all Lapses |

A divine quest doesn't count as your Great Deed for the floor. You can do both.

### Failing a Divine Quest: the Smite

**A failed divine quest is always punished.** Your god smites you with its **Apostate's Smite** (see `pantheon`), and you stay its worshipper. The smite's damage depends on the quest, not your rank:

| Stars | Smite damage | Lasting effect lasts | Also |
|-------|--------------|----------------------|------|
| ★ | 2d10 | 1 hour | Lose 2 Favor |
| ★★ | 4d10 | Until your next long rest | Lose 3 Favor, gain 1 Lapse |
| ★★★ | 8d10 | Until the end of the floor | Lose 5 Favor, gain 1 Lapse |
| ★★★★ | 12d10 | Until the end of the floor | Lose 8 Favor, gain 1 Lapse |
| ★★★★★ | 16d10 | Until the end of the next floor | Lose 12 Favor, gain 2 Lapses |

- **No saving throw**, as for any smite. The smite can't take you below 1 hit point unless you are at **Wrath** (4 Lapses) or worse.
- **Abandoning** a divine quest is failing it.
- **Leaving your god** with a divine quest open fails the quest too. You take both smites. Gods are thorough.
- **If your god is slain** while you have its divine quest, the quest just ends. No reward, no smite.

## Quests on Your Character Sheet

The **Quests** page of the Crawler Character Sheet (and the Quests tab of the Crawler Sheet app) is a simple list. For each quest, write what it is, who it's from, the reward promised, and any notes (deadlines, steps, questline links, what your god will do if you fail). Tick the box when it's done.

## Designer Notes

- Ordinary quests are carrots, not sticks. The fun of failing a quest is seeing what the world does next, not losing hit points.
- Divine quests are the exception, on purpose. Worshipping a god is a deal, and the god has the bigger lawyer.
- The penalty rule (penalties are always shown up front) keeps the System fair. Hidden consequences come from the world, never from the System.
