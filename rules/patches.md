# Patch Notes

> *"Hotfix 3.1.7 is now live. The interaction between Grease, a shopping trolley and a staircase has been corrected. Crawler Dave, we know it was you. Congratulations on your Bug Bounty. Please stop."*

The World Dungeon is run by **the System**: an alien game-running AI that builds the floors, spawns the monsters, counts the viewers and writes every snarky message you have ever received. It is very good at its job. It is not perfect. Crawlers are endlessly creative, and some of the things they do were never meant to be possible.

When the System finds a problem, it **patches the game**. Rules change. Items get rebalanced. Monsters get retuned. Sometimes in the middle of a fight.

At the table, this is the GM's tool for fixing the game while you play it. A broken combo, a rule that doesn't work, an encounter that is far too hard or far too easy: the GM does not have to stop the session to argue about it. The System notices, the System fixes it, and the System is rude about it. This chapter is how that works, and the promises the System makes about it.

---

## What the System Patches

| Problem | What a patch does | Example |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| **Exploit** | An unintended combo or trick that breaks the game | Infinite gold from selling and rebuying a cursed item |
| **Bug** | A rule that doesn't work, contradicts another, or has no answer | Two features that each say they go last |
| **Balance** | Something far too strong or far too weak | A weapon trait that nobody would ever take |
| **Encounter** | A fight, trap or floor that is badly tuned | A boss that the party kills in one round, or can't scratch |
| **Quality of life** | Bookkeeping that slows the table down | Merging three trackers into one |

The System patches **the game**, not the players. It never patches away a clever plan that worked once, a lucky roll, or a crawler's good idea. If it was fun and it didn't break anything, it is a **feature**.

---

## How Patches Arrive

### Hotfix (right now)

For something that is breaking the game this minute.

1. **The world freezes.** Time stops for everyone on the floor. A System message appears: *"HOTFIX INCOMING. Please remain calm. Do not touch anything."*
2. **The GM says what changes.** One short sentence, in the System's voice.
3. **The world resumes.** Play continues from exactly where it stopped. Nothing that already happened is undone.

Use a hotfix rarely: at most **once a session**, and only when waiting would spoil the game.

### Patch (at the stairs)

Most changes wait. When crawlers take the stairs to a new floor, the System posts **Patch Notes** with the floor's welcome message: a short list of what changed, with a comment for each. Patches can change anything, from one item to a whole rule.

### Known Issues

When the GM spots a problem but isn't ready to fix it, the System can post it as a **Known Issue**: *"We are aware that Mother Moldra accepts a slightly old sandwich as her Rite. A fix is coming."* The thing still works until the patch. Use as much of it as you can before then. The audience loves a crawler racing a patch.

## The System's Promises

The System is petty, but it plays fair. Every patch keeps these promises.

- **No rollbacks.** A patch never undoes what already happened. Kills stay killed. Loot stays looted. XP stays earned.
- **No punishment.** A crawler who found an exploit is never punished for it. Using something the System built is not cheating. The System calls it "unauthorized creativity", and pays for it (see *Bug Bounty*).
- **Compensation.** If a patch weakens something a crawler chose or paid for (a feat, a Crawler Skill, a weapon trait, an item), that crawler gets a **Respec**: they can swap that choice for another legal one, free, at the next safe room. Gold and materials spent on it are refunded in full.
- **Legacy items.** Instead of a Respec, a crawler can keep an item that was nerfed exactly as it was. It is now marked **Legacy**. It works the old way for its owner only, and can't be copied, crafted or sold for more than its new value.
- **Buffs are free.** If a patch makes something stronger, everyone who has it gets the buff at once.
- **Everyone, everywhere.** A patch applies to every crawler, monster and NPC in the dungeon, not just the party. If the System fixes an exploit for you, it fixes it for the boss too.
- **It tells you.** A patch is always announced. The System never changes a rule in secret.

## Bug Bounty

Finding an exploit is an achievement.

| Achievement | Trigger | Tier | Box | The System says |
|-------------|---------|------|-----|-----------------|
| **Bug Bounty** | Find something the System has to patch | Notable | Fits the exploit | "Thank you for your report. We hate you." |
| **Zero Day** | Find an exploit that needs a hotfix | Major | Fits the exploit | "Do you know how many engineers we had to wake up?" |
| **Patch Rider** | Get real use out of a Known Issue before it is fixed | Minor | Mystery | "Enjoy it while it lasts." |
| **Speedrunner** | Use an exploit to skip a whole fight or floor | Major | Fits the exploit | "That's not how you're supposed to play. It was beautiful." |

Each counts once per patch, not once ever. The first crawler in the dungeon to find a bug gets the better reward, and the System names and shames them to everyone.

An exploit also earns **Audience points** as normal (see `surviving-the-crawl`). Viewers love a crawler who breaks the game.

## For the GM

The patch rules exist so that a mistake in the rules, or a ruling made in a hurry, never has to stay in your game forever. They also mean you can say "yes" to a strange idea at the table, knowing you can fix it later if it turns out to be a problem.

- **Rule at the table, patch later.** When a question comes up mid-fight, make a quick ruling and keep playing. If the ruling turns out wrong, patch it at the stairs.
- **Patch the problem, not the player.** If one crawler keeps finding exploits, that is a crawler having fun. Reward it, then fix the hole.
- **Talk first, in real life.** Before a patch that changes a player's character, tell that player out of character. The System can be smug in the game; you shouldn't be at the table.
- **Small and few.** One or two changes per floor is plenty. A game that changes every session stops feeling like a game.
- **Patch monsters too.** If an encounter is badly tuned, the System can retune it mid-fight with a hotfix: *"Balance adjustment: Hobgoblin Warlord hit points increased. We underestimated you. It won't happen again."* Use it in both directions.
- **Write it down.** Keep a list of every patch in your campaign notes, with the version number and the floor it went live on. Give each one a number like **1.4.2**: the floor, the patch on that floor, and the hotfix.
- **Patch the books too.** If a rule in these handbooks doesn't work for your table, change it with a patch. Your Patch Notes are your house rules, and the System will take the blame.

### Patch Notes Example

These changes are made up, to show the style. They are not real rules changes.

> **WORLD DUNGEON PATCH 4.0.0 — Floor 4 is live**
>
> - **Grease** no longer turns shopping trolleys into cavalry. *"We admire the ambition."*
> - **Heavy Hands** now says when the extra dice apply. *"Several crawlers misread it in their favor. All of them."*
> - **Highlight Reel** (weapon trait) now recharges on a short rest. *"Someone please pick this."*
> - **Known Issue:** Thrumm accepts a Grudge List entry for a creature that only wronged you by existing. *"Fix coming in 5.0.0."*
> - **Bug Bounty** awarded to Crawler Dave (Silver Demolitionist Box). *"Please stop."*
