# Safe Rooms and the Floor Timer

> *"Attention, Crawlers. This floor will collapse in 12 hours. Anyone still on it will be recycled. Have a nice day."*

Every floor of the World Dungeon is on a clock. When the clock runs out, the floor **collapses**, and anyone still on it dies. To survive, you must find a **stairwell** and go down before time runs out. Along the way, **safe rooms** are the only places where you can truly rest.

---

## Floor Turns

Time on a floor is tracked in **floor turns**. One floor turn is about **1 hour** of dungeon time. The GM marks off floor turns as you explore, fight and rest. Combat still uses normal 6-second rounds; a whole fight only costs one floor turn.

| Activity | Floor turns |
|----------|-------------|
| Explore a new area (a few rooms, a street, a cave) | 1 |
| Travel back through areas you have already explored | 1 per 2 areas |
| A fight, including looting the bodies | 1 |
| Search an area thoroughly, or harvest monster parts | 1 |
| Short rest | 1 |
| Shop, sell loot, and open boxes in a safe room | 1 |
| Craft at a safe room workbench | As the recipe (see the *Crafting and Shops* handbook) |
| Long rest (safe room only) | 8 |

The GM can adjust these for big or small areas. The point is simple: everything you do spends time you do not get back.

---

## Resting

### Long Rests

**You can only take a long rest inside a safe room** (or a private safe room from a Safe Room Key).

If you try to sleep anywhere else, you gain no long-rest benefits. You get the benefits of a short rest at most, and the GM makes an Ambush Check (below) for every 2 floor turns you spend there.

### Short Rests

You can take a short rest anywhere, but outside a safe room you might be found.

**Ambush Check.** At the end of a short rest outside a safe room, the GM rolls a d20. If the roll is **equal to or lower than** the area's danger number, mobs find you. The rest is interrupted: you gain no benefits from it, and a fight begins.

| Area | Danger number | Chance |
|------|---------------|--------|
| Quiet (a cleared dead end, a hidden nook) | 4 | 20% |
| Normal (most of a floor) | 8 | 40% |
| Dangerous (a mob nest, a busy street) | 12 | 60% |
| Boss territory | Automatic | 100% |

**Modifiers**

| Situation | Change to the danger number |
|-----------|-----------------------------|
| The party hides the camp (group Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 13, succeeds) | −3 |
| You barricade the only entrance | −2 |
| Someone keeps watch (no benefit from the rest for them) | −2, and no surprise if mobs come |
| You made loud noise in the last floor turn (explosions, gunfire) | +4 |
| The floor timer has 6 floor turns or fewer left | +4 |

## Safe Rooms

Safe rooms are the System's rest stops: small, warm and strictly neutral. Every floor has several. Mark them on your map when you find them.

### What You Can Do in a Safe Room

- **Long rest.** The only place you can.
- **Open loot boxes.**
- **Shop and sell.** Most safe rooms have a shop with a System-run shopkeeper. Monster parts, scrap, junk and unwanted loot sell here. See the *Crafting and Shops* handbook for shop types, stock and prices.
- **Craft.** Every safe room has a free workbench. Powerful items (uncommon explosives and firearms, magic items) can only be crafted here.
- **Eat, wash and recover.** Hot food, a bathroom, and a bed. Some safe rooms have a bar and a bartender who knows things.
- **Meet other crawlers.** Safe rooms are shared, and gossip travels.

### Safe Room Rules

- **No violence.** Mobs cannot enter. Crawlers cannot attack anyone inside. A crawler who tries is thrown out of the safe room and cannot return to it for 8 floor turns. The System also tells everyone.
- **No boss fights.** Bosses, and anything chasing you, stop at the door.
- **The clock keeps running.** You can stay as long as you like, but the floor timer does not stop. The audience gets bored if you hide too long (−1 Audience point per 24 floor turns spent in safe rooms in a row).
- **Closing time.** When the floor timer has **6 floor turns** left, safe rooms close to new arrivals. At **1 floor turn** left, everyone inside is pushed out. Safe rooms do not save you from the collapse.

## The Floor Timer

Every floor has a **floor timer**: the number of floor turns before it collapses. The timer starts when you arrive on the floor.

| Floor | Floor timer | About |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| 1–2 | 48 floor turns | 2 days |
| 3–4 | 72 floor turns | 3 days (Floor 3 includes class selection) |
| 5–6 | 96 floor turns | 4 days |
| 7–8 | 120 floor turns | 5 days |
| 9–10 | 144 floor turns | 6 days |
| 11–12 | 168 floor turns | 7 days |
| 13–14 | 192 floor turns | 8 days |
| 15–16 | 216 floor turns | 9 days |
| 17 | 240 floor turns | 10 days |
| 18 | Special | The final floor has its own rules |

**Floors grow.** The timer gets longer every two floors because the floors get bigger and more complex: more neighborhoods, more bosses, longer routes between safe rooms, and stairwells that are harder to reach. A longer timer is not a gift. There is more to do, and more of it wants you dead.

### Warnings

At the start of each dungeon day (every 24 floor turns), the System announces how many days are left. It also announces the time left at **24**, **12**, **6** and **1** floor turns. In the last floor turn, it counts down out loud.

### Collapse

When the timer reaches 0, the floor collapses. **Any crawler still on the floor dies.** There is no save. Crawlers inside a stairwell are safe.

> **GM option: Last Chance.** For a less brutal game, a crawler caught in the collapse makes a DC 20 Constitution saving throw. On a success, they are dumped onto the next floor at 1 hit point, with no Audience points and one random inventory item lost. On a failure, they die.

## Stairwells

Each floor has a few **stairwells** down to the next floor. They are hidden, guarded, or far apart, and finding one is often a quest in itself. Mark them on your map when you find them.

- **Entering a stairwell is safe.** Mobs cannot follow you in, and the collapse cannot reach you there.
- **You can wait inside.** You can take short rests in a stairwell without Ambush Checks, but not long rests.
- **It's one-way.** When you walk down the stairs, you arrive on the next floor and its timer starts. You can never go back up.
- **Party travel.** If your party enters the stairwell together, you arrive together.

---

## Going Down Early

You can take the stairs whenever you find them. But leaving early has costs.

**You miss the grind.** Every floor turn you leave behind is a fight, a box or a level you did not earn. The next floor is built for crawlers who are 3–4 levels stronger than when they arrived on this one. Arrive under-leveled, and the floor below will punish you.

**You miss the bosses.** Neighborhood and floor bosses give Boss Boxes and big achievements. They do not follow you down.

**The audience feels cheated.** Viewers want to see you earn it. When you go down, you lose Audience points based on how much of the timer was left:

| Timer left when you descend | Audience points |
|-----------------------------|-----------------|
| More than 75% | −3 |
| 50–75% | −2 |
| 25–50% | −1 |
| Less than 25% | 0 |
| The final floor turn | **+1**, and the *Just in Time* achievement |

> **The real decision.** Every floor turn spent grinding makes you stronger. Every floor turn spent grinding also brings the collapse closer. Leave too early and the next floor eats you. Leave too late and this one does.

## Running the Clock (GM)

The clock only adds pressure if it stays light to run. These habits keep it from slowing the game down.

1. **One clock, the GM's.** The GM keeps the real timer on the **GM Floor Tracker**. Players can copy it on the **Floor Timer** page of their character sheet, but the GM's sheet is the truth.
2. **Tick at the end of scenes, not actions.** When a scene ends, ask "how long did that take?" and cross boxes from the cost table. Never count minutes during a scene.
3. **Use fixed costs.** A fight is 1 floor turn whether it lasted 2 rounds or 20. A long rest is 8. No arguing, no maths.
4. **Keep it on the table.** Put the tracker where players can see it, or say the time left out loud after every scene ("40 turns left"). Visible pressure does the work for you.
5. **Only the warnings matter.** Players do not need the exact number all the time. Read the pink boxes (24, 12, 6, 1) out as System announcements, and let the fear do the rest.
6. **Rows are days.** Each row on the tracker is 24 floor turns, one dungeon day, split into four 6-turn watches. Write one line per day in the day log. That is your floor's story, and it makes recaps easy.
7. **Fast-forward the boring parts.** When the party wants to grind, use **Grinding Blocks** (below) instead of playing every fight.
8. **Rests are the big chunks.** Most of the timer goes on long rests. When the party asks "can we rest?", the real question is "can we afford 8 turns?". That is the decision the clock exists to create.

### Grinding Blocks (optional)

When the party wants to grind mobs for levels and loot, they can spend a **block of 6 floor turns** instead of playing out each fight.

- The GM makes one Ambush Check for the area, to see whether something serious turns up. On a hit, play out one real fight.
- Otherwise, each crawler rolls once on the Enemy Loot table for the area's usual creatures, and gains **150 XP** on Floors 1–6, or **125 XP** from Floor 7 (see `leveling`).
- Each crawler loses hit points equal to 2 × their level (they can spend Hit Dice afterwards as normal).
- Grinding is boring television: no Audience points are gained during a grinding block.

## Designer Notes

- **Open questions for playtest:** Are the floor timers too long or too short for 3–4 levels per floor? Deeper floors have much longer timers, so they need enough content (neighborhoods, bosses, quests) to fill them. Is the Ambush Check too punishing at 40% for normal areas?
- **Tracking.** The Crawler Character Sheet (Floor Timer page) and the GM Floor Tracker put all of this on paper.
- **Floors 18 and beyond.** The final floor needs its own design.
